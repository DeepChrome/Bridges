# The cast of Bridges

*Written for: anyone who writes a scene, a line, a prompt or a picture for
Bridges — the owner, a model, or an engineer. The data lives in
`core/cast.js`; this is how to use it.*

Bridges has a story running under the lessons. Every conversation, every
picture and every guide moment is an episode in the life of one small dog
and his family, with one small monkey forever trying to spoil it.

## The family

**Тедди — Teddy** (a Yorkshire terrier, he). The main character and the
guide on every screen. Tiny, with a face like a baby Yoda: huge dark eyes,
big fringed ears, golden face, silver body. Curious, brave, cheerful, a bit
proud of his hair, always hungry. He learns by trying things. He forgives
Monka every time. Voice: Puck.

**Нежа — Nezha** (a rabbit, she). Teddy's mother. Beautiful and glamorous,
warm and loving, confident, quick-witted — the one with the plan and the
last word. Her name is short for Нежана, "tender", and echoes Coneja.
Voice: Aoede.

**Ярик — Yarik** (a wolf, he). Nezha's husband, Teddy's father. Big, grey,
fearsome to strangers and a complete softie at home. Calm, protective, dry
jokes, unable to hide how proud he is. Short for Ярослав. Voice: Charon.

## The rival

**Монька — Monka** (a monkey, he). The cosmonaut monkey in a space suit a size
too big. Small, goofy, a little bit evil, always sneaking around with a
scheme to spoil the family's plans — out of jealousy, because he has no
family making a fuss of him. **Every scheme turns round on him**, the way
the coyote's do: the trap catches Monka, the stolen cake was the one he
needed, the prank lands on his own helmet. Voice: Fenrir.

The rules for Monka, because this is the part that can go wrong:

- **Unlucky, never humiliated.** The joke is the backfire, not Monka being
  small or stupid. Nobody mocks him; the family usually does not even know
  what he tried.
- **He has a heart.** Now and then a scene ends with the family inviting him
  in, or with Monka doing something kind that nobody sees. Not every time —
  the running gag has to keep running.
- **He is never cruel.** His schemes are mischief: hiding the keys, swapping
  the sugar and salt, a banana peel, a fake map.

## The neighbourhood

The avatar characters (`core/avatars.js`) live next door and fill the other
roles in a scene — shopkeeper, doctor, teacher, driver. They come and go;
the four above are the story.

- **Белка — Belka**, a red squirrel, Nezha's best friend. Quick, chatty,
  knows everyone's news, hides snacks everywhere. Voice: Zephyr.
- **Тортила — Tortila**, the old wise turtle. Slow, patient, never wrong.
- **Гена — Gena**, the grumpy crocodile at the corner shop, which has a few
  café tables at the back — so a scene can buy from him or eat at his place.
- **Лиса — Lisa**, a sly fox; sometimes Monka's partner in a scheme, and
  quicker than him to switch sides.
- **Миша — Misha**, a big warm bear in a winter hat.

The other avatars (Borsuk, Filin, Barsik, Tsarevna, Pirozhok, Senya, Zaya,
Serafim) are learners' faces, not story characters, unless a scene needs a
walk-on.

## How to use them

- **A conversation** is an episode: two or three of these characters in a
  real everyday situation the chapter's words can carry (buying bread, a
  lost key, a trip to the doctor). The family's plan is the situation;
  Monka's scheme, when he appears, is the complication, and it backfires by
  the end. Not every conversation needs Monka — about half.
- **Teddy is in most scenes**, but the learner must hear a range of voices:
  Nezha and Belka are the two women's voices, Yarik and Monka the two men's
  besides Teddy.
- **A picture** shows the family doing the unit's topic (playing football
  in Sport, cooking in Food), with Monka somewhere in the frame and his plan
  going wrong.
- **The tutor** (Talk, the personal tutor) may speak as one of them when a
  situation needs a character; it never needs to explain who they are.
- **Names decline** like any Russian name: Нежа → Нежи, Неже, Нежу, Нежей;
  Ярик → Ярика, Ярику, Яриком; Монька → Моньки, Моньке, Моньку, Монькой; Белка → Белки,
  Белке, Белку, Белкой. **Тедди never changes.**
- Animals doing human things is the point; nobody in a scene remarks that a
  wolf is married to a rabbit.
