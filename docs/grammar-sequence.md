# Grammar sequence

The order in which Bridges introduces grammar, and why. Decided 2026-09-06 from
what the established courses agree on; the owner's brief was "less complicated to
more complicated" and a spine a learner can leave for side quests and come back to.
Revisited 2026-11-06 ("make sure they are taught extremely well and in an order
that makes sense"): the reference now follows this order too (core/grammar.js).

## What the courses do

| Source | Order of the major points |
|---|---|
| Cornell *Beginning Russian Grammar*, grammar reviews I–XIV | nominative vs accusative, pronouns → **accusative, gender, adjectives, present/past verbs** → **prepositional** (location vs motion), conjugation classes → nominative plural, **genitive**, animacy, aspect intro → **dative**, prepositions → **instrumental** → case endings, spelling rules → aspect pairs → reflexives, imperative, time → numerals → motion verbs, conditionals → comparatives, prefixes → participles |
| *The New Penguin Russian Course* (N. J. Brown) | lesson 4 present tense and the idea of case → lesson 5 **prepositional** → lesson 6 **accusative** and going places → … participles and verbal adverbs in lessons 27–28 |
| Duolingo Russian (95 units, 2025 data) | intro: introductions, basic sentences, **plurals**, **where you are** (prepositional), trips → A1: **genitive**, possession, numbers, likes, **past**, family, weather, months, clothes → A1 cont.: **dative**, **instrumental**, genitive plural, reflexives, commands, relative clauses, future → A2: prefixes, participles, abstract topics |
| RussianForFree basic course (14 lessons) | first words → **present tense** singular/plural → conjugations → **past** → reflexives → possessives & adjectives → cases intro → **aspect** → imperative → future → motion verbs |
| TORFL A1 (ТЭУ) requirements | alphabet; **nominative, prepositional, accusative**; genitive and dative introduced; gender agreement; **present and simple past**; basic aspect pairs (брать/взять, говорить/сказать); basic motion verbs; numbers and time |
| Red Kalinka / howtogetfluent A2 description | all six cases met by the end of A2, prepositional and accusative first, common prepositions; numbers with cases; time expressions |

Consensus, in order: no articles / no "is" → gender and agreement → present-tense
verbs (two conjugations) → plural → prepositional (location) → accusative (object,
direction) → past tense → genitive (possession, absence, quantity) → dative (likes,
age) → instrumental (with, by, as) → aspect → future → reflexives → imperative →
motion verbs → numerals → comparatives → conditionals → participles.

Two things every course does that are worth copying:

- **The prepositional comes first** of the five oblique cases, because it is one
  ending (-е) answering one question (where?). A grammar book lists the cases
  nominative, genitive, dative…; a course never teaches them in that order.
- **A form is met as a phrase before it is met as a rule**: «мне нра́вится»,
  «у меня́ есть», «два часа́» are taught as set phrases weeks before the dative
  and genitive are named.

## What Bridges does

Ten chapters. Each spine unit carries one card; side quests carry one each and
may lean on a later case only as a set phrase the card names as such.

| Chapter | Spine card | Side-quest cards (required; *optional*) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No "a", no "the", no "is" | family: «my» takes the noun's gender · food: saying what you want (хоте́ть) |
| 2 | Two verb patterns (present tense) | tech: мочь / хоте́ть + a verb · home: asking yes or no |
| 3 | Every noun has a gender; adjectives copy it | city: «this shop» and «this is a shop» · time: numbers change the noun (set phrases) |
| 4 | Making things plural | travel: идти / ходить · speech: по-ру́сски · clothes: adjectives in the plural |
| 5 | Saying where something is (prepositional) | work: having and not having (**genitive**) · emotion: liking is done to you (**dative**) · *business*: how much it costs |
| 6 | The accusative marks the object | body: what hurts is the subject · animals: animate accusative · *science*: заниматься + instrumental |
| 7 | The past tense agrees with the subject | school: three ways to study · nature: в лесу́, на мо́ре · *art*: о + prepositional |
| 8 | Two aspects of every verb | sport: playing, and doing sport (**instrumental**) · *politics*: quantity words take the genitive |
| 9 | Talking about the future | medicine: у врача́, в больни́це · *religion*: ве́рить в / ве́рить + dative |
| 10 | Asking someone to do something | *law*: what someone is or became (instrumental) · *military*: с + instrumental |

The one gap the 2026-11-06 review closed: the instrumental was taught only on
optional quests, so a learner on the required route never met it on a card.
Sport (chapter 8, required) now carries it. And family's card used to say "the
reason comes with gender in chapter 3"; it gives the reason where «мой» and
«моя́» are first met.

## The reference

Practice → The rules lists the same order, grouped by chapter: first sentences,
gender, the present tense, adjectives, numbers, the plural, hard and soft, going
somewhere, the six cases (prepositional first), prepositions, pronouns, past,
aspect and future, building sentences. Every case and paradigm is shown on real
words with the ending coloured, and every point that turns on one difference has
a contrast: two sentences that differ in exactly that.
