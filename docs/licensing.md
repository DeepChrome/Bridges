# Audio licensing audit (ROADMAP P8.1) — 2026-09-07

What the app ships, where it came from, what the rights are, and what a public
release would need. Counts are from `data/audio.json` on this date (13,180
utterances mapped to 13,368 files). This is an audit, not legal advice; the
decision is the owner's and the roadmap marks it **[STOP — user decides]**.

## What is shipped, by source

| Source | Utterances | What it is | Rights as far as they can be read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Russian Core 5000 | 10,311 (78 %) | Audio bundled with a shared Anki deck ("Russian Core 5000", 10,000 cards). Uniform 64 kbps mono; whether human or TTS is unverified (ROADMAP §11). | **Unknown, probably not redistributable.** The deck's author packaged the audio; nothing in the deck states a licence for it, and the recordings may themselves be taken from a commercial course. The sentence texts are a separate question: the owner's own cards are his; the
Tatoeba sentences are CC BY 2.0 FR and are already credited (§30a); OpenRussian is
CC BY-SA 4.0 and credited (rule 20.10). The video library embeds YouTube players
and ships no caption text, only timings and the owner's own curriculum words
(P8.8 answered: `payload.videos` holds `{t, w, s}` moments where `s` is a ten-word
window of caption text around the word — **that is caption text and would have to
go for a public release**; strip `s` to the spoken form only, or keep it under
fair-use-sized snippets. The build constant is `VIDEO_MOMENTS`; the snippet is cut
in `build_transcripts.py snippet()`).

## Options

**(a) Synthetic-first, clean.** Keep Tatoeba CC BY/CC0 (159). Regenerate every
other utterance with a TTS the owner licenses himself — Yandex SpeechKit (the
current Yandex files show what it sounds like) or Google Cloud TTS Neural2/Wavenet
— and label every one "synthetic" in the app, as the device voice is labelled now.
Cost: ~13,000 utterances × ~40 characters ≈ 520k characters. Google Cloud TTS
Wavenet is $16 per million characters after a 1M free tier a month → **$0**; Yandex
SpeechKit is ~₽0.15 per 15 characters → **~$5–10**. Work: one script (a sibling of
`build_audio.py`), one day; the honesty rule holds because the label is on every
file (`src` in `audio.json` already carries the source letter).

**(b) License Core 5000 audio.** Find the deck's author and the recordings'
origin; if it is a commercial course, license it. Cost unknown, likely
**hundreds to thousands of dollars** and weeks of correspondence, with no guarantee
the author can grant what he never held.

**(c) Record replacements.** ~4,000 studied words and ~1,500 pooled sentences by a
native speaker: at a freelance rate of ~$0.10–0.20 per word for reading and
editing, **$1,500–3,000** and several weeks. Best sound, highest cost.

**Recommendation:** (a) for a release, keeping Tatoeba's real voices where they
exist and marking the rest synthetic. Nothing changes for the owner's own use in
the meantime; the current build is a private tool.

## What is cheap regardless of the option (P8.3)

Gate the 31 Tatoeba recordings that are NC, ND or unstated behind a build flag so
a public build drops them: `fetch_tatoeba_audio.py` already reads the licence; the
filter belongs in `build_audio.py`'s manifest step. Two hours. Not done in this
pass — the owner decides first, and the private build may keep them.
